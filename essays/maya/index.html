Looking Inward

MAYA

A memory from Tirupati, a restaurant called Maya, and a question about how much of life we spend looking outward rather than within.

Through My Quiet Lens3 min read
Looking InwardAttentionReflection

I remember one particular visit to Tirupati.

For those who have never been there, seeing Lord Venkateswara is not simply a matter of walking into a temple.

You wait.

In the 1990s, I remember waiting close to twelve hours for darshan.

Twelve hours in a slow-moving queue, for a few seconds in front of the deity.

We used to go to Tirupati often, so this was not unusual for us.

On one such visit, after the darshan, we came back down to Tirupati and stopped for lunch at a restaurant called Maya.

There was a placard there explaining the name.

It said, in essence, that Maya is that which is not really there, and yet appears to be there.

Illusion.

I don’t remember the exact words anymore.

But I remember the thought.

It stayed with me.

Later, I came across another idea that somehow connected with it.

Think about our senses.

Our eyes can see something miles away.

But they cannot see what is happening just behind them, inside our own body.

Blood can move through the aorta at more than a metre a second.

We don’t feel it.

Our heart beats roughly a hundred thousand times a day.

We don’t hear most of them.

Put a stethoscope against the chest, and suddenly you realise the sound was there all along.

That always fascinated me.

Our senses are incredibly powerful.

But almost everything about them seems to point outward.

See there.

Hear that.

Touch this.

The outside world keeps asking for our attention.

Inside is different.

There is no eye that tells us what we are afraid of.

No ear that tells us what we are avoiding.

No sense organ that tells us why something still bothers us, why we want what we want, or why we keep holding on to certain things.

For that, we have to stop and look in a different direction.

And I find it interesting that this thought began for me in Tirupati.

I had spent close to twelve hours waiting to see God for a few seconds.

Then I walked into a restaurant called Maya.

Maybe that is why the word stayed with me.

We trust what we can see.

We react to what we can hear.

We spend most of our lives responding to what is outside us.

But perhaps that is only one part of what is real.

There may be another part, much closer, that we barely notice.

Maybe Maya is not only about what is not there.

Maybe it is also about how easily we miss what is.
